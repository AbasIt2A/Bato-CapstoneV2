# CKD Stages

## Stages of CKD

Doctors describe CKD in five stages, based mainly on the eGFR — a number
(estimated from a creatinine blood test, plus age and sex) that says how
well the kidneys are filtering (KDIGO, NIDDK).

The amount of albumin (a protein) in the urine also matters: more albumin
means higher risk of the disease getting worse (KDIGO).

Only a doctor, using laboratory tests, can determine someone's stage.
Earlier stages usually have no symptoms at all.

### Stage 1

eGFR 90 or higher — kidneys filter normally, but there are signs of damage
(such as protein in the urine).

### Stage 2

eGFR 60-89 — mildly reduced filtering, with signs of damage.

### Stage 3 (3a and 3b)

eGFR 30-59 — mild-to-moderate loss of function; many people are first
diagnosed at this stage.

### Stage 4

eGFR 15-29 — severely reduced function; this is when doctors start planning
for possible kidney failure treatment.

### Stage 5

eGFR below 15 — kidney failure (also called end-stage kidney disease);
dialysis or a kidney transplant is usually needed to live.
