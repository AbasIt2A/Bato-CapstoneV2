# How CKD Is Defined and Classified

## The definition of chronic kidney disease

CKD is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for a
minimum of 3 months, with implications for health (KDIGO, 2024).

## The two criteria that establish CKD

Either of the following, present for at least 3 months, meets the criteria for
CKD (KDIGO, 2024):

- **A marker of kidney damage** — albuminuria (ACR of 30 mg/g or more),
  abnormalities in the urine sediment, persistent blood in the urine,
  electrolyte abnormalities due to tubular disorders, abnormalities seen on
  biopsy or on imaging, or a history of kidney transplantation.
- **Decreased filtering** — a GFR below 60 mL/min/1.73m².

## Why three months matters

The three-month rule is what makes kidney disease *chronic*. A single abnormal
eGFR or ACR result should not be assumed to be chronic, because it can instead
be the result of a recent acute kidney injury. Chronicity is established by
reviewing past measurements, by repeat measurements at and beyond the
three-month point, by imaging, by medical history, or by kidney biopsy
findings (KDIGO, 2024).

This is why a single test result is not a diagnosis, and why a health care
provider will usually repeat an abnormal test before concluding anything.

## The CGA classification: cause, GFR and albuminuria

CKD is classified on three things together, abbreviated CGA: the **C**ause,
the **G**FR category (G1 to G5), and the **A**lbuminuria category (A1 to A3).
All three are needed to judge how severe a person's CKD is and what their risk
is (KDIGO, 2024).

A point often missed is that the *definition* of CKD includes many markers of
kidney damage, while the *classification* system uses only the two dimensions
of GFR and albuminuria (KDIGO, 2024).

## The albuminuria categories A1, A2 and A3

Albuminuria is graded into three categories by the urine albumin-to-creatinine
ratio (KDIGO, 2024):

| Category | ACR (mg/g) | ACR (mg/mmol) | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | under 30 | under 3 | Normal to mildly increased |
| A2 | 30–300 | 3–30 | Moderately increased |
| A3 | over 300 | over 30 | Severely increased |

A2 was formerly called "microalbuminuria" and A3 "overt proteinuria"; the
category names replaced those older terms.

## Testing for CKD uses both markers together

People at risk of CKD, and people who have it, should be tested using **both**
a urine albumin measurement and an assessment of GFR — not one or the other.
If an elevated ACR, blood in the urine, or a low eGFR turns up incidentally,
the tests should be repeated to confirm that CKD is present (KDIGO, 2024).

## Finding the cause of CKD

Establishing the cause of a person's CKD matters, because it is what allows
management to be tailored to them. The cause is worked out from clinical
context, personal and family history, social and environmental factors,
medications, physical examination, laboratory measures and imaging (KDIGO,
2024).

## Why "kidney function" and "GFR" are not the same phrase

KDIGO asks that "GFR" be used for the specific filtering function it measures,
and the broader phrase "kidney function" be kept for the totality of what the
kidneys do — filtering, blood pressure control, red blood cell signalling,
bone health and blood chemistry (KDIGO, 2024).
