# Signs of the Conditions in BATO's Kidney Screening

## Signs of anemia

Anemia means having too few healthy red blood cells. Anemia linked to
chronic kidney disease usually develops slowly and may cause few or no
symptoms in early kidney disease (NIDDK).

When symptoms appear, they may include tiredness, weakness, shortness of
breath, unusually pale skin, dizziness or fainting, headaches, body aches,
a fast or irregular heartbeat, trouble sleeping, trouble concentrating, and
chest pain (NIDDK).

Chest pain that will not go away, or difficulty breathing, needs emergency
medical care right away (NIDDK).

These signs have many other causes. Having them does not mean a person has
anemia — only a doctor and a blood test can tell.

## Signs of high blood pressure

Most people with high blood pressure (hypertension) have no signs or
symptoms, so many people do not know they have it. The only way to know is
to have your blood pressure measured (CDC).

Very high blood pressure together with chest pain, shortness of breath, back
pain, numbness, weakness, a change in vision, or difficulty speaking is an
emergency and needs emergency care right away (CDC).

## Signs of diabetes

Diabetes can start with few or no symptoms. Some people with type 2 diabetes
do not notice any symptoms at all (CDC).

When symptoms appear, they may include peeing often, feeling very thirsty or
hungry, losing weight without trying, tiredness, feeling irritable or moody,
blurry vision, and frequent urinary tract or yeast infections. Type 2
diabetes can also cause cuts or sores that heal very slowly, dark patches of
skin around the neck, armpits, or groin, and numbness or tingling in the
hands or feet. Type 1 diabetes can also cause nausea, vomiting, and stomach
pain (CDC).

These signs have many other causes. Only a doctor and a blood sugar test can
tell whether a person has diabetes.

## Warning signs of a heart attack

The major warning signs of a heart attack are chest pain or discomfort,
often in the center or left side of the chest, lasting more than a few
minutes or going away and coming back; feeling weak, light-headed, or faint,
sometimes with a cold sweat; pain or discomfort in the jaw, neck, or back;
pain or discomfort in one or both arms or shoulders; and shortness of breath.
Some people, more often women, also have unusual tiredness, nausea, or
vomiting (CDC).

Anyone with these warning signs needs emergency care immediately (CDC).

## Warning signs of a stroke

The warning signs of a stroke come on suddenly: numbness or weakness in the
face, arm, or leg, especially on one side of the body; confusion, trouble
speaking, or difficulty understanding speech; trouble seeing; trouble
walking, dizziness, loss of balance, or lack of coordination; and a severe
headache with no known cause (CDC).

An easy way to remember them is BE FAST: Balance, Eyes, Face, Arms, Speech,
Time. Anyone with these signs needs emergency care right away, even if the
signs go away after a few minutes (CDC).

## Signs of heart failure

Common symptoms of heart failure include shortness of breath during daily
activities, trouble breathing when lying down, weight gain with swelling in
the feet, legs, ankles, or stomach, and generally feeling tired or weak
(CDC).

These signs have many other causes. Only a doctor can tell whether a person
has heart failure.

## Signs of peripheral vascular disease

Peripheral vascular disease includes peripheral arterial disease (PAD), poor
blood flow to the legs. Its classic sign is pain, aching, or cramping in the
legs — the buttock, hip, thigh, or calf — during activity such as walking,
which gets better with rest (CDC).

Other signs in the legs and feet can include weak muscles, hair loss, smooth
and shiny skin, skin that feels cool, weak or missing pulses in the feet,
sores that do not heal, and cold or numb toes. Up to 4 in 10 people with PAD
have no leg pain (CDC).

Only a doctor can tell whether a person has peripheral vascular disease.

## Signs of protein in the urine

Most people with protein in the urine (proteinuria or albuminuria) do not
notice any symptoms (NKF).

When symptoms are present, they may include foamy urine, puffiness around
the eyes (especially in the morning), peeing more often than usual, and
swelling of the feet, ankles, belly, or face (NKF).

Only a urine test can show protein in the urine, which is why regular
check-ups with lab tests matter (NKF).
