# Kidney Tests and What the Results Mean

## Why testing is the only way to know

Early kidney disease usually has no symptoms, so testing is the only way to
know how well the kidneys are working. The sooner kidney disease is found, the
sooner treatment can start to help protect the kidneys (NIDDK).

## The two tests used to check for kidney disease

Health care providers use two tests. The first is a blood test that checks how
well the kidneys are filtering the blood, called GFR (glomerular filtration
rate). The second is a urine test that checks for albumin, a protein that
passes into the urine when the kidneys are damaged. The same two tests are
used afterwards to monitor kidney disease and check that a treatment plan is
working (NIDDK).

## What GFR means

GFR stands for glomerular filtration rate, and it describes how much blood the
kidneys filter each minute. A GFR of 60 or more is in the normal range. A GFR
of less than 60 may mean kidney disease is present. A GFR of 15 or less is
called kidney failure, and most people below this level need dialysis or a
kidney transplant (NIDDK).

GFR cannot be raised, but it can often be kept from going lower (NIDDK).

## What creatinine is

Creatinine is a waste product from the normal breakdown of muscle in the body,
and healthy kidneys remove it from the blood. Providers use the amount of
creatinine in the blood to estimate GFR. As kidney disease gets worse, the
creatinine level goes up (NIDDK).

Estimated GFR is more accurate than serum creatinine on its own, because
creatinine alone is affected by muscle mass and by the related factors of age,
sex and race (NIDDK).

## What albumin in the urine means

Albumin is a protein found in the blood. A healthy kidney does not let albumin
pass into the urine; a damaged kidney lets some through. Having albumin in the
urine is called albuminuria, and the less albumin in the urine, the better
(NIDDK).

## The two ways urine is checked for albumin

A **dipstick test** uses a strip of chemically treated paper placed into a
urine sample; the strip changes colour if albumin is present.

A **urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR)** measures and compares the amount
of albumin with the amount of creatinine in the sample, which lets a provider
estimate how much albumin would pass into the urine over 24 hours. Unlike a
dipstick, UACR is not thrown off by how dilute or concentrated the urine is
(NIDDK).

## What a urine albumin result means

A urine albumin result of 30 mg/g or less is normal. More than 30 mg/g may be
a sign of kidney disease and is the marker used to identify albuminuria. If
albumin is found, a provider may repeat the urine test once or twice more to
confirm the result (NIDDK).

## How to tell whether kidney disease is getting worse

Test results can be tracked over time. Treatment is working if GFR stays the
same and urine albumin stays the same or goes down. A health care provider
works with the person to manage their kidney disease (NIDDK).

## Who should get checked, and how often

People with diabetes should get checked every year. People with high blood
pressure, heart disease, or a family history of kidney failure should ask their
health care provider how often they should be tested (NIDDK).
