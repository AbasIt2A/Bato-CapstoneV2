# Symptoms, Causes, and Complications of CKD

## Symptoms of CKD

Early CKD (stages 1-3) usually causes NO symptoms — that is why screening
and testing matter (CDC, NIDDK).

In later stages, possible symptoms include: swelling in the legs, ankles,
feet, or around the eyes; tiredness or weakness; poor appetite; nausea;
trouble sleeping; urinating more or less than usual; foamy urine; dry and
itchy skin; muscle cramps; and trouble concentrating (NIDDK).

These symptoms can have many other causes — having them does not mean a
person has CKD, and not having them does not rule CKD out. A doctor and
laboratory tests are the only way to know.

## What causes CKD

Diabetes and high blood pressure are the two leading causes (CDC, NIDDK).

Other causes include: glomerulonephritis (inflammation of the kidneys'
filtering units), polycystic kidney disease (an inherited condition where
cysts grow in the kidneys), long-lasting blockage of the urinary tract
(for example from kidney stones or an enlarged prostate), repeated kidney
infections, and long-term overuse of pain relievers like NSAIDs (NIDDK).

## Health problems CKD can cause (complications)

- Anemia — damaged kidneys make less of the hormone that triggers red blood
  cell production (NIDDK).
- Bone and mineral problems — kidneys help balance calcium and phosphorus;
  CKD can weaken bones over time (NIDDK, KDIGO).
- Heart and blood vessel disease — the most common serious complication of
  CKD (KDIGO, CDC).
- High potassium — can disturb the heart's rhythm and is dangerous (NIDDK).
- Fluid buildup — swelling and higher blood pressure as kidneys struggle to
  remove extra water (NIDDK).
