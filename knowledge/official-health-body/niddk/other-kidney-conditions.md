# Other Kidney Conditions

## What is nephrotic syndrome?

Nephrotic syndrome is a group of signs that the kidney's filters are leaking
too much protein into urine. It can involve foamy urine, puffy eyelids or
swelling in the legs and feet, and low protein in the blood. Diabetes, lupus,
and several kidney diseases can cause it. A health professional checks urine
protein and may use blood tests, imaging, or a kidney biopsy to find the cause.
Treatment depends on that cause and the amount of kidney damage. These symptoms
alone cannot identify nephrotic syndrome.

Source: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/nephrotic-syndrome-adults

## How can lupus affect the kidneys?

Lupus nephritis is kidney inflammation caused by lupus, an immune-system
condition. It may cause foamy urine, swelling, or high blood pressure, but a
person cannot tell from symptoms alone whether their kidneys are affected.
Health professionals use urine and blood tests and sometimes a kidney biopsy
to diagnose it and guide treatment. Treatment may include medicines that calm
the immune system and protect kidney function; the choice is made by a health
professional. Early evaluation and treatment may help protect the kidneys.

Source: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/lupus-nephritis

## What is IgA nephropathy?

IgA nephropathy, also called Berger's disease, happens when an immune protein
called IgA builds up in the kidney's tiny filters and damages them. Possible
signs include pink or cola-colored urine, foamy urine, and swelling, but these
signs occur in other conditions too. Some people have no noticeable symptoms
for years. Health professionals check urine and blood, and a kidney biopsy can
confirm the diagnosis. Treatment aims to reduce blood pressure and protein
loss and protect kidney function; it varies by the person's findings.

Source: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/iga-nephropathy
