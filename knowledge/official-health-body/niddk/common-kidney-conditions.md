# Common Kidney Conditions Beyond CKD and Stones

## Kidney infection and urinary tract infection

A kidney infection (pyelonephritis) is a type of urinary tract infection that
often begins in the bladder and spreads to one or both kidneys. Possible signs
include fever or chills, pain in the back or side, frequent or painful
urination, and nausea or vomiting. These symptoms need prompt medical care:
an untreated kidney infection can become serious. A clinician may use a urine
test and culture, and sometimes imaging, to find the cause. Bacterial kidney
infections are treated with prescribed antibiotics; a blockage such as a stone
may also need treatment. Symptoms alone cannot confirm the diagnosis.

## Polycystic kidney disease (PKD)

PKD is an inherited disorder in which many fluid-filled cysts grow in the
kidneys. It differs from a simple kidney cyst and can reduce kidney function
over time. The common autosomal dominant form may not cause symptoms at first;
possible signs include back or side pain, high blood pressure, and blood in
the urine. A clinician considers family history and imaging, and may use
genetic testing, when diagnosing PKD. Care focuses on monitoring kidney health
and managing complications such as high blood pressure. A person with a family
history or possible symptoms should discuss testing with a health professional;
one cyst or one symptom does not establish PKD.

## Glomerular disease and glomerulonephritis

Glomerular diseases damage the kidneys' tiny blood filters (glomeruli);
glomerulonephritis is a form involving inflammation. Damage may let protein or
blood leak into urine. Possible signs include foamy or pink-brown urine,
swelling around the eyes or in the legs, and high blood pressure, though some
people have no symptoms. Some forms follow an infection or involve an immune
problem. A clinician may check urine and blood tests and sometimes perform a
kidney biopsy to identify the cause. Treatment depends on that cause and the
extent of kidney damage; symptoms or urine appearance alone cannot identify a
specific glomerular disease.

## Blood in urine (hematuria)

Hematuria means blood in the urine. It may be visible as pink, red, or brown
urine, or found only on a urine test. Possible causes include urinary
infection, kidney stones, glomerular disease, and other conditions, including
urinary tract cancers. The color or presence of blood cannot tell which cause
is responsible. A clinician may repeat a urine test and use history, an exam,
imaging, or other tests to investigate. Blood in urine warrants a medical
evaluation, particularly if it is visible, persists, or occurs with pain,
fever, or trouble urinating.

Source: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/hematuria-blood-urine

## Simple kidney cysts

Simple kidney cysts are fluid-filled sacs that are usually harmless and often
found during imaging for another reason. They are different from inherited
polycystic kidney disease: simple cysts generally do not enlarge the kidneys
or reduce kidney function. Many need no treatment. Occasionally a cyst causes
pain, blocks urine flow, becomes infected, or bleeds; fever, pain, or blood in
urine should be discussed with a health professional. Imaging and, when
needed, blood or urine tests help distinguish a simple cyst from other kidney
problems. A person cannot determine the type of cyst from symptoms alone.

Source: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/simple-kidney-cysts
