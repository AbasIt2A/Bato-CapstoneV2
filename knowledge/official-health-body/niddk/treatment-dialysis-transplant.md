# Treatment, Dialysis, and Transplant

## How CKD is treated

CKD usually cannot be cured, but treatment can slow or stop it from
getting worse, especially when started early (NIDDK, WHO).

The core of treatment is controlling the cause: keeping blood pressure
and blood sugar in the target range the doctor sets (KDIGO, CDC).

Doctors may prescribe medicines that protect the kidneys and treat
complications. Which medicine and what dose is strictly the doctor's
decision — BATO never advises on specific medicines.

Regular monitoring (eGFR, urine albumin, blood pressure) tracks whether
the disease is stable or progressing (KDIGO).

People with CKD should avoid medicines and supplements that can harm the
kidneys — including regular NSAID use and herbal products that claim to
"cleanse" the kidneys — unless their doctor approves them (NIDDK).

## Treatments for kidney failure (stage 5)

- Hemodialysis — a machine filters waste from the blood, usually done
  several times a week at a dialysis center (NIDDK).
- Peritoneal dialysis — uses the lining of the belly to filter blood, and
  can often be done at home (NIDDK).
- Kidney transplant — a healthy kidney from a donor; for many people this
  offers the best long-term health and quality of life (NIDDK).
- Conservative (supportive) care — for some, managing symptoms without
  dialysis is a valid choice made with doctors and family (KDIGO).

Choosing among these is a personal medical decision made with a
nephrologist (kidney specialist).
