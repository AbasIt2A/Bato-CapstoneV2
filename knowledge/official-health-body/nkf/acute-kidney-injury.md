# Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

## What acute kidney injury is and possible signs

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden loss of kidney filtering ability that
develops over hours or days. It is different from chronic kidney disease,
which develops over a longer period, although AKI can raise the risk of later
chronic kidney disease. Possible signs include much less urine than usual,
swelling, fatigue, nausea, shortness of breath, or confusion. Some people have
no symptoms and AKI is found on blood tests. These signs have many possible
causes and cannot establish AKI on their own. Markedly reduced urination,
breathing difficulty, or confusion needs urgent medical assessment.

Source: https://www.kidney.org/kidney-topics/acute-kidney-injury-aki

## Causes, tests, and treatment of acute kidney injury

AKI can follow serious illness, dehydration or low blood flow to the kidneys,
urinary blockage, or exposure to certain medicines, including some pain
relievers. A clinician reviews recent illness and medicines, checks blood
creatinine and urine, and may use imaging to find the cause. Treatment depends
on the cause and may include fluids, treating infection or blockage, adjusting
medicines, and sometimes temporary dialysis. Do not stop a prescribed medicine
or start an over-the-counter pain reliever solely on the basis of general
information; ask a health professional for advice about your situation.
Follow-up kidney testing is important after an episode of AKI.
