# Which Drinks Help or Harm the Kidneys

## Why what you drink matters to the kidneys

The kidneys are the body's natural filtration system: they remove waste,
balance minerals and help maintain blood pressure (NKF).

Staying properly hydrated helps the kidneys flush waste out through urine, keep
blood pressure at a healthy level, balance electrolytes such as sodium,
potassium and calcium, and reduce the risk of kidney stones and infections
(NKF).

Not drinking enough fluid over a long period can lead to kidney damage. But
hydration is not only about drinking MORE — it is about drinking the right
fluids (NKF).

## How much to drink is individual, not a fixed number

There is no one-size-fits-all rule for how much a person needs to drink in a
day (NKF).

What a person needs depends on their age, body type and size, climate,
activity level, whether they are pregnant or breastfeeding, and health
conditions — including vomiting, diarrhoea, or kidney disease requiring
dialysis (NKF).

Ask a health care professional what a healthy hydration plan looks like for
you. Someone with advanced CKD or on dialysis may be given a fluid LIMIT rather
than a target, and that limit is set by their care team.

## Drinks that are kidney-friendly

NKF names four good options (NKF):

- **Water** — no calories, no sugar, no additives, and without doubt the best
  choice for kidney health. The kidneys use water to filter waste, and drinking
  enough also helps prevent kidney stones and urinary tract infections, both of
  which can make kidney problems worse. If plain water feels boring, try
  infusing it with fruit, herbs or spices.
- **Unsweetened fruit juices**, in moderation. Many store-bought juices are
  loaded with sugar and additives that can hurt the kidneys — look for juice
  with no added sugar.
- **Plant-based milks**, choosing an unsweetened variety with no added
  phosphorus. Many contain less potassium and phosphorus than dairy milk, but
  they are not all the same, so read the label.
- **Dairy milk**, which is a good source of calcium and protein for bones and
  muscles. A person with kidney disease may need to limit how much milk they
  drink, because phosphorus and potassium can build up in the body.

Low-sodium broths and foods with a high water content also contribute to
hydration for most people (NKF).

## Soft drinks and soda

Regularly drinking soda can raise a person's risk of diabetes and high blood
pressure — the top two risk factors for kidney disease (NKF).

Dark cola specifically is made with phosphoric acid for preservation and taste.
Phosphoric acid may cause kidney damage, especially in people who already have
kidney disease (NKF).

A study based on participants' self-reported intake suggests that regularly
drinking cola was associated with a higher risk of kidney disease (NKF).

Instead of soda, NKF suggests carbonated water sweetened with fruit (NKF).

## Energy drinks

Energy drinks may boost energy briefly, but they can also damage kidney health
(NKF).

They are ultra-processed foods, full of additives with little or no nutritional
value — a lot of caffeine, added sugar, and additives including phosphorus and
sodium. Research has linked a high intake of ultra-processed foods to an
increased risk of kidney disease (NKF).

NKF's substitution is plain: drink a glass of water. Water can help fight
fatigue if a person is not well hydrated (NKF).

## Alcohol

Drinking too much alcohol dehydrates the body and forces the kidneys to work
harder (NKF).

Over time, excessive alcohol consumption may contribute to high blood pressure,
liver disease and kidney damage (NKF).

As alternatives, NKF suggests infused water, herbal teas or fresh fruit juices.
A person who already has kidney disease should ask a kidney dietitian what is
right for them (NKF).

## Sweet drinks

Sugary drinks such as lemonade and sweet tea are not good choices for kidney
health (NKF).

A 2024 study based on participants' self-reported intake suggests that
regularly drinking sugar-sweetened OR artificially sweetened beverages was
linked to an increased risk of developing kidney disease — that is, switching
to a diet version is not established as a solution (NKF).

Better options are unsweetened fruit juice in moderation, or a small amount of
juice mixed with water (NKF).
