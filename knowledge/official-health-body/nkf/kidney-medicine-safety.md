# Medicines and Kidney Safety

## Pain medicines with kidney disease

Acetaminophen (also called paracetamol) is generally safe for the kidneys at recommended doses, but taking too much can harm the liver. Check cold and pain product labels because several products may contain it. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), including ibuprofen and naproxen, can reduce blood flow to the kidneys and cause acute kidney injury or worsen chronic kidney disease, especially with higher doses or longer use. People with CKD should ask a health professional before taking an NSAID. The safest choice depends on the cause of pain, kidney and liver health, and other medicines; do not use this general guidance as a personal prescription.

## Antibiotics and other medicines when kidney function is reduced

When kidney function falls, some medicines stay in the body longer and may cause more side effects. Many antibiotics and other medicines for infections are cleared through the kidneys, so a clinician may adjust the medicine or schedule using kidney function (eGFR). An infection still needs proper treatment; a person with CKD should ask their prescriber or pharmacist whether the prescribed medicine and dose fit their kidney function. Not every medicine requires a change. During acute kidney injury or dehydration, the care team may temporarily adjust other medicines too. Do not change doses or stop prescribed medicines without asking the care team.
